Our Mission

Pillars of Road & Public Transit Reform

Combining boots-on-the-ground education with legislative pressure and infrastructure oversight.

1. Public Transit Standards

Advocating for mandatory speed governors, strict bus overloading prohibitions, and enforced rest cycles for long-distance drivers across primary inter-city corridors.

2. Safe Pedestrian Infrastructure

Demanding raised pedestrian crosswalks, protected walkways, and continuous fencing around school zones and high-density market centers like Lusaka's Great East Road.

3. Transparent Enforcement

Campaigning for digital speed monitoring networks and anti-corruption body-worn cameras for traffic enforcement units to eliminate arbitrary stops and ensure real driver accountability.

How We Create Change

Nationwide Advocacy & Training Methods

We take road safety straight into school classrooms, bus stations, and community halls to build localized safety culture across every Zambian province.

School Safety Clubs

Establishing student-led Road Safety Clubs in primary and secondary schools. We teach pupils defensive pedestrian habits, safe street crossing techniques, and school zone hazard recognition.

Community Workshops

Hosting interactive town-hall sessions in urban compounds and rural transit hubs to equip residents, parents, and community leaders with basic bystander safety protocols and rights awareness.

Driver Sensitization

Collaborating directly with public transport unions at bus stations to deliver peer-to-peer workshops on defensive driving, fatigue management, and passenger care.

Civic Campaigns

Distributing educational materials, running radio awareness programs, and coordinating public petitions that hold local councils accountable for unlit or hazardous roads.

Commuter Protection

Know Your Rights as a Passenger

Public transport users hold the power to demand basic safety standards inside minibuses, inter-city coaches, and taxis.

Your Rights On Board

  • The right to travel without vehicle overloading or improper seating arrangements.
  • The right to demand the driver adhere to legal speed limits.
  • The right to clean, operational seatbelts in long-distance inter-city buses.
  • The right to speak out or request a safe stop if a driver shows signs of intoxication or fatigue.

Operator Accountability

  • Public service vehicles (PSVs) must display valid RTSA fitness and registration credentials clearly.
  • Commercial drivers must hold valid, unexpired PSV license endorsements.
  • Operators must honor posted passenger fare rates without intimidation.
  • Buses must not drop passengers at undesignated, dangerous roadside locations along major corridors.
Legislative Action Plan

Key Policy Reform Priorities

We present these core policy initiatives to regulators, public transport unions, and transport authorities to drastically reduce highway fatalities in Zambia:

1. Automated Speed Networks

Deploy permanent, automated speed cameras along high-fatality stretches like the Lusaka-Ndola highway corridor.

2. Mandatory Driver Fatigue Controls

Enforce mandatory electronic logbooks for commercial inter-city bus operators to eliminate long, continuous shifts.

3. Universal Crash Survival Training

Require basic first-responder survival protocols and post-crash triage training as part of all commercial PSV licensing.
